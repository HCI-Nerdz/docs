# Prompt seed — Context Edge

**Date:** 2026-08-10  
**Origin:** Author framing (conversation seed) before curated essay/docs  
**Public face:** [Context Edge](https://hci-nerdz.github.io/docs/hci-nerdz/context-edge.html) · [Ecosystem nav at the screen edge](https://hci-nerdz.github.io/blog/ecosystem-nav-at-the-screen-edge/)

---

I've noticed Google's cloud console and many of their other apps follow a design philosophy of embedding new platforms within the entrypoint platform, without properly separating the interface design language… they have the new platform sort of overload the old platform, instead of separating the controls to be clear what platform is providing the current context.

I think a more intelligent and sophisticated way to deal with context problems, at least on desktop, would be to have the top edge, or left edge, or top and left edges simultaneously for either the same or different types of context, provide a context navigation system, which reveals when clicking the edge. The edge should get bigger on hover to show enough space to fit in a label to explain what it does (or in a mature system, no need for any sort of labeling: the mere presence of this bar, with a color or dark/white color, would suggest it).

The different platforms or apps within the ecosystem should assume a different base color that looks like an overlay on the ultimate base color, so that users can visually associate their activities in a subplatform or sub-app with a stable non-competing visual representation. They could even involve some duo tone scheme or radial gradients to further increase the amount of ways to differentiate and quickly identify which platform is active.

I believe this idea should become a standard pattern in web apps and websites that have subsections or sub-roles (reddit, stackoverflow, microsoft, etc). Microsoft has a huge ecosystem, and knowing how to navigate it is difficult. This idea allows users to summon a wireframe navigation interface between different platforms/modes, etc, so users don't have to keep relearning how to navigate within an ecosystem.

Clicking the context bar would open up a full interface for nav, instead of overloading the responsibility of the website header/top bar. Designers keep putting subsections in the navbar at the top, and it's fucking ugly and difficult to use, because it's ephemeral, and it's illogical because the nav shows on top of the content, when the nav is really the substrate for access to different activities.

For example, cities have streets and skyscrapers. The streets don't get summoned on top of the skyscrapers. The skyscrapers are features and the streets are the nav substrate, at the bottom.
